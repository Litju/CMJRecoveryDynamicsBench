A controlled synthetic study

From Force Plate to Forecast

Modeling CMJ performance changes following training

We define what a countermovement jump (CMJ) measures, model how that measurement changes after a training exposure, describe nine neural network families, and test four reference forecasters on simulated data.

No athlete was measured. Every number traces to the public, version-controlled study results.

2 · Mathematical model

How the simulated jump changes after training

A^{\text{true}}_{\text{criterion}}=A^{\text{true}}_{\text{baseline}}\,\bigl(1+r(\tau)+e\bigr) r(\tau)=-a_f\,e^{-\tau/24\,\text{h}}-a_s\,e^{-\tau/84\,\text{h}}
  • 24 h and 84 h are fixed modes of the synthetic generator: modeling assumptions, not measured human recovery constants.
  • Amplitudes are bounded (af 0.01–0.08, as 0–0.08 of baseline) and vary with exposure, participant sensitivity and camp; e is a bounded discrepancy.
  • The forecast targets are the change at 24, 48 and 72 h.

3 · Neural architectures M0–M8

Nine original network families

Each family changes one structural element. Two recorded training repeats per family: 18 networks in total.

Not scored. M0–M8 were not part of the four-model experiment on the next pages. No NRMSE exists for them, and they are not ranked.

Inputs: 39 raw features, 4 × 4 prior-record values, horizon, 5 public-schedule basis values. The basis uses fixed 36 h and 120 h decays: architecture-side constants, not the generator's 24 h / 84 h modes. Two outputs: ΔF (N/kg), ΔJ (m/s).

5 · Results

Forecast error on synthetic data

Lower is better. NRMSE = RMSE ÷ SD of the true change, per cell.

  • Ridge was lowest in every root; the structured comparator, built on the generator's own modes, did not beat it.
  • Fitted-model error rose with horizon. Synthetic error only: not athlete accuracy, not scores for M0–M8.

6 · Interpretation

What the evidence supports

Each model's paired difference from the zero baseline was entirely below 0 in every root. Current exposure and prior history carried the usable signal in this generator; participant context added almost nothing. Error barely moved from 24 to 96 training camps.

Not shown. No ridge-versus-structured interval was recorded, so "lower in each root" is descriptive, not a tested difference. No predictive validity for athletes, and no external generalization: one generator, three seeds.
