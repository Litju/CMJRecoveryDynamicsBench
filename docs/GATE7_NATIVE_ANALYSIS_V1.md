# Gate7 native analysis, version 1

`metrics.native_analysis` is a synthetic-only analysis implementation. It preserves the separately versioned native measurement/identity contract; it does not use generic canonical targets, calibrate a historical score, authorize empirical execution, or load checkpoints. A caller declaration cannot establish that records really are synthetic. Both the evaluator and training-constant constructor reject a non-synthetic dataset kind, even if supplied permission decisions claim approval. A future empirical path requires separate review and authorization.

The plan must explicitly lock its primary candidate, four target/horizon rules with native units, RMSE improvement margins, maximum RMSE/MAE and absolute signed-bias bounds, minimum participant counts, analysis unit, confidence-interval method, multiplicity family, test method/assumption, random seed, resamples and exclusions. There are no default scientifically meaningful margins. Rehearsal thresholds are hypothetical engineering inputs, with no clinical or human-validity interpretation. Every candidate requires repeats r0 and r1 on exactly the same query/target rows. Preflight stages for structure, measurement authority, independence, use, result generation and result disclosure must all pass and the preflight receipt must be pinned. The analysis admission receipt binds the exact dataset, source authority, participant splits, native observations and inspected preflight payload fingerprint; a receipt for another file is rejected. These are separately reviewed declarations, not permissions inferred from a valid file.

## Metrics and comparisons

The four separate cells are force in N/kg and impulse in m/s at H72 and D7. Signed error is prediction minus observed additive innovation. The query-weighted estimates are RMSE, MAE and signed bias, accompanied by cell participant/query counts. No averaging across incompatible units occurs. Each repeat is reported separately; the family estimate is the arithmetic mean of the two per-repeat metrics, never an ensemble of their predictions. Repeat disagreement is descriptive, not a predictive interval.

Zero innovation is the prespecified primary comparator. An optional separate constant artifact can be fitted only from supplied training-group observations. The version-2 artifact binds four ordered native cell means, source authority, the exact designated training participants, the complete training observations including labels, and the training-only arithmetic-mean protocol. Its content hash detects local inconsistency; it is not independent authentication. Validation/test labels are refused by that fitting entry point; the analysis requires a separately trusted `expected_constant_baseline_sha256`, checks that expected identity in addition to local content consistency, and requires the exact designated training groups. Substituting values and recomputing the artifact hash cannot replace that expected pin. The trusted caller must obtain the pin from the approved fitting step or an authenticated fit receipt, never from the incoming artifact. Missing pins and old version-1 artifacts are refused. The engineering rehearsal uses a separately frozen pin for its invented training fixture. All fitting and qualification here use invented values only. No historical OLS comparator is silently introduced.

## Paired uncertainty and hypothesis test

A locked NumPy PCG64 generator first draws a `(resamples, participants)` array of participant indices with replacement, then, only above 16 participant groups, a same-shaped array of -1/+1 signs for the separate hypothesis test. Participant identities are sorted. The bootstrap retains every query, origin, horizon, target, candidate, repeat and baseline of each drawn participant. Repeated draws multiply the participant's contribution; clusters with more queries retain their larger query weight. Every comparison uses the identical draw array. At least 10,000 resamples are required.

For every repeat and family, 95% two-sided marginal percentile confidence intervals use NumPy's linear quantile convention. Paired contrasts include candidate-minus-baseline RMSE, MAE and bias; RMSE improvement is baseline-minus-candidate. The family metric is recomputed as a mean of per-repeat metrics in every resample. Confidence intervals are not declared simultaneous or predictive.

The prespecified one-sided participant sign-flip test uses participant sums of zero-baseline squared error minus the mean of the two repeat squared errors. Its null is zero paired squared-error improvement, under independent participant clusters and symmetry of the cluster loss difference under the null. It is not a test of a nonzero RMSE margin and is not distribution-free without that assumption. For 16 or fewer participant groups, enumerate all `2^n` sign allocations and report `count(statistic >= observed) / 2^n`, without a plus-one correction. The bound limits the sign matrix to 65,536 × 16 int64 values (8 MiB); the transient arithmetic arrays remain bounded. For larger cohorts retain sampled allocations and the plus-one Monte Carlo correction `(1 + count(randomized statistic >= observed statistic)) / (B + 1)`. Receipts record exact versus Monte Carlo mode, allocation count, seed (unused for exact testing), enumeration bound and limitations. Monte Carlo tails remain seed-dependent approximations with resolution `1/(B+1)` and possible repeated allocations. A bound derived from float64 epsilon, participant count and absolute loss magnitude conservatively includes numerical ties; the observed and randomized statistics use the same reduction convention.

Methodological finding: the old p=1 shortcut for nonpositive observed statistics was conservative but was not the full right-tail probability. Both modes now count that same prespecified right tail, including at zero and negative observations; the null, statistic and scientific decision thresholds are unchanged. All-zero losses still give p=1. With six nonzero positive group improvements the smallest exact p is 1/64=0.015625, so the four-cell Holm minimum is 4/64=0.0625 and cannot meet alpha 0.05. Exact p values are discrete and independent of the random seed; bootstrap intervals retain their separately locked seed.

Holm adjustment covers the locked primary candidate's four zero-improvement tests. Other candidate comparisons are exploratory. Each primary cell must additionally have a paired RMSE-improvement CI lower bound strictly greater than its prespecified margin, RMSE/MAE upper bounds within the harm limits, its bias interval inside the signed bounds, enough participants and an adjusted p-value <=0.05. All four cells must pass. Failure is reported explicitly; an engineering fixture can correctly produce `criteria_not_met`. The test and margin guard have different documented roles. This implementation does not infer power or research success from synthetic outcomes.

## Reproduction and verification

Run `python -m cmj_recovery_dynamics.metrics.native_rehearsal --synthetic-rehearsal` outside or inside an installed environment. It creates a new, explicitly invented labeled fixture; admits it through preflight; fits an invented training-only constant; pairs 18 candidate/repeat combinations; computes four-cell analysis; and exercises refusal controls. Predictions are intentionally constructed from invented labels and errors to test arithmetic; they are not evidence of prediction performance. No recovered weights or original records are used.

The JSON receipt includes source/contract identities, plan/input/prediction hashes, counts, exclusions, RNG and quantile conventions, all contrasts, cell-level decisions and an explicit false empirical-execution authorization. It contains no timestamp, so identical inputs/environment produce identical receipts. Tests independently expand sampled participant rows, including unequal cluster sizes and repeated participant draws, and compare numerical metrics/intervals; known Holm examples and sign-flip tie cases are also checked. Receipt hashes establish byte consistency, not real-world scientific provenance.
